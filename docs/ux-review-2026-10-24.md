# UX review, 2026-10-24: consistency, flow and copy

Founder, 2026-10-24: "Let's do the UX review, for consistency and especially flow, and also copy", with four examples that feel off, and a sense that they are not isolated. **They are not.** Each of the four is one case of a pattern that shows up in many places. This review finds the patterns, so fixing a rule fixes every case, rather than fixing four screens.

## How this was done
A script signed up a new business and walked the app on a phone viewport (light mode), building real data on the way: a business, materials, a product with sizes, an extra, steps and costs, a customer, a quote with a discount and a deposit, mark as sent, accepted, a job and its counts, an invoice, a payment sheet, a credit note sheet, the statement, Home. It saved a full-page screenshot and the page's text for each of about 55 screens. I read all the text (copy), looked at the screenshots (layout and flow), and checked the code behind every case. Screenshots: `/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-makerflow/8539b6b1-3c1c-5130-b554-078363749515/scratchpad/audit/` (numbered in the order walked; the name says what each is).

**Not covered:** dark mode and desktop width (spot checks only), the theme studio, invoice drafts, the Terms page's example flow, the customer's details form. They are in the second pass, if you want one.

## The four examples, and what they really are
| You said | It is one case of | Theme |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Add a discount is an optional button, but a deposit is a checkbox in a section" | Optional additions to a document are revealed **five different ways** | **T1** |
| "In the extras flow there's a confusingly worded checkbox in a checkbox" | Conditional controls built from stacked checkboxes, worded as a rule instead of a choice | **T2** |
| "Bank details wording confuses other ways to pay with notes on paying" | The same printed text has **different names on different screens**, and some names say the wrong thing | **T3** |

## T1. Optional things are revealed five different ways
On the quote form alone, every one of these is "something you may add":
| Thing | How it is offered today |
| --- | --- |
| Discount | A `+ Add a discount` button; opens a section with a select |
| **Deposit** | A **section that is always there, with a checkbox** "Ask for a deposit to start work"; ticking it opens fields |
| Delivery or collection | Three radio buttons, always shown ("Not decided yet" is one) |
| Needed-by date | A `+ Add a needed-by date` button inside Dates |
| Terms | A `+ Add a term` button, and a "Customer agrees to the terms" card |
| Bank details | A `+ Add bank details` button |
| Notes, Sign-off, Description, Notes about paying | Always-visible optional text boxes, each "(optional)" |
| A customer's address | `+ Add address, delivery details or notes` |

On the same screens a choice between alternatives is a radio card (Variations: No/Yes; Steps: As a whole/As steps), and a plain on/off setting is a checkbox ("Show archived", "Include on new quotes", "I'm registered for VAT"). The **settings** page for the same Deposit and Discount is yet another control (a select: "No deposit / I decide on each quote / A percentage / A fixed amount").

**Why it hurts.** A maker cannot predict how to turn something on, how to turn it off again, or whether it is on. The form is 12 sections and about 9,850 px tall on a phone; most of that is things that are off.

**Related flow bug.** Open a Discount or Deposit and leave it empty, then Save: the save is refused ("Discount: Enter a percentage") for something you opened by accident. (`38-FAIL-quote-save-preview.png`, from the script doing exactly that.)

**Proposed rule.** *An optional addition is a `+ Add …` button that opens its section; the section has a Remove; an opened section left empty is dropped quietly on save. A choice between alternatives is radio cards. A real on/off setting is a switch.* Applied: Deposit becomes `+ Add a deposit` (like Discount); Delivery or collection, Needed-by, Terms, Notes, Sign-off, How to pay follow the same rule on the quote, the invoice and the job forms. **Bigger option:** group them under one "Add to this quote" tray of buttons below Items, so the form shows what is on, not what could be, and shortens by half.

## T2. Checkboxes inside checkboxes, worded as rules
- **Item sheet, an extra:** `☑ Custom Ribbon  +R5 each` then, inside it, `☐ A set number, not one for each item`, then a stepper "Custom Ribbon: how many". Two levels of checkbox, and the inner label states the exception ("not one for each item") instead of the choice. The founder's screenshot.
- **Product, extras editor:** `☐ Ask for wording` ("Typed on the quote, like what to engrave"), `Where does it apply?` as a select of "Any product: one price everywhere / This product only", and **"What it costs you"** with three add buttons, all inside one card under a card summary that repeats the extra's name twice.
- **Documents → Terms:** a checkbox "Include on new quotes" beside a "Coming soon" card "Only on certain products".
- **Job page:** every counted step has a stepper **and** an "All done" checkbox.

**Proposed rule.** *One control per question, named for the answer.* For an extra on a quote: "How many? ( ) One for each item ( ) A set number [ 1 ]". "Ask for wording" becomes "Customer types a note (like what to engrave)". "Where does it apply?" becomes "Offer it on: All my products / Only this one".

## T3. The same printed text has different names
| What is printed | Called |
| --- | --- |
| The line at the top of a quote | **Introduction** (Quotes and invoices) and **Description** (the quote form); a third place, "Description", on products and items means something else |
| The closing line | **Sign-off message** (settings) and **Sign-off → Message** (quote) |
| **How to pay: other ways to pay** | **"Notes about paying"** in settings *and* on the quote, with examples "SnapScan, PayShap, or 'pay on collection'". Those are **other ways to pay**, not notes |
| Text for the customer under the items | **Notes** → "Notes for your customer" |
| Text for you | **Your note** (job), **Note** (invoice) and **Note (optional)**, where the invoice's is for the customer |

**Why it hurts.** The maker meets "Notes about paying" under Bank details and reads it as "notes on this payment", when the box is for SnapScan or a collection note. And "Notes", "Notes about paying" and "Note" are three boxes on one quote.

**Proposed vocabulary.** *Intro* (top), *Notes* (under the items, for the customer), *Other ways to pay* (under the bank details), *Sign-off* (the closing line), *Private note* (only you). The same words in settings and on the document. Rename the examples hint: "Printed under your bank details, like SnapScan or pay on collection."

## T4. Caveats stated everywhere, whether or not they apply
"**without VAT**" is printed **unconditionally**, for a business that is not VAT-registered too, in: the Inputs tab subtitle ("What goes into making one batch, without VAT. Only you see this."), the footer of the costs summary ("Costs and margins are worked out without VAT."), the extras costs ("What goes into one, without VAT. Only you see this."), the item sheet's cost hint ("Optional, without VAT. Only you see this."), the hourly rate ("Without VAT."), the margin ("What is left of the price, without VAT, after costs.") and the quote's margin panel. "**Only you see this**" appears six times. For a maker who is not registered, none of it means anything; for one who is, it is said four times on one screen.

**Other boilerplate in the same family:** "Existing quotes are not changed / never changed" (three places), "Sent quotes keep the details they were sent with", "Used only to show fitting examples first" (repeated on two screens), "Anything in [square brackets] … not legal advice" on every term screen, a 45-word paragraph under the Deposit settings.

**Proposed rule.** *Say a caveat once, only where it changes what the maker does, and only to the maker it applies to.* Concretely: say nothing about VAT in costs unless the business is VAT-registered, then one line on the Inputs tab; replace the repeated "Only you see this" with one small **Private** tag beside the section title; shorten the settings explanations to one idea each (under 15 words); keep the legal-advice note once per Terms screen.

## T5. Long pages made of many separate forms
| Page | Phone height | Separate Save buttons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business profile | about 10,100 px | **6**: Save details, Save logo, Save hourly rate, Save margin, Save what you make, Save bank details |
| Quotes and invoices | about 9,200 px | **7**, labelled differently: Save quote wording, Save deposit, Save discount, **Save**, Save quote numbers, Save invoice numbers, Save credit note numbers |
| New quote | about 9,850 px | 2 (Save draft, Continue) over 12 sections |

Buttons are left-aligned and small on some cards and full width on others. "What your time is worth" and "The margin you aim for" are **pricing preferences** sitting in the **Business profile**, which is meant for facts about the business.

**Proposed.** Group each page into 3 to 4 sections with the same tab bar as the product form (Business profile: *Business | Pricing | Payments*; Quotes and invoices: *Wording | Defaults | Numbering | Terms and themes*), one consistent "Save" per card (same label, same position, full width on a phone), and move the pricing preferences out of the profile.

## T6. Flow
1. **Home** for a new business offers one thing: a "Your jobs" card with *New job*. The first thing a maker does is a **quote**, and nothing on Home says so. After a first quote there is no pipeline either: Home shows jobs and "Money owed", not "quotes waiting for an answer", "accepted, not yet invoiced" or "paid this month".
2. **An accepted quote** (`42-quote-accepted-s1.png`) has a "Create a job?" card with three buttons, then a full-width primary "Download PDF", then "What you can do next": *Change the answer* (first, as a button), a primary *Create invoice*, *Quote again*. That is three primary-coloured actions of different weight, and the correction is listed before the next step. After the job exists the same page changes shape.
3. **An invoice** repeats itself: the top card shows Total and Owing, and the Payments card below shows "Paid so far / Owing" again; two buttons, *Record the deposit* and *Record a payment*, with the second always available.
4. **A job's note** needs its own **Save note** button on a page where every count saves as you tap. The job's "No day set" is text with no way to set it from there.
5. **Credit note sheet:** two labels both say "Why" (*Why*, and *Why it is being credited*); the list of items reads "1 and R 1 354,50 left to credit" (1 what?); "Credit everything left" is an unlabelled shortcut between the fields.
6. **Item sheet:** the subtitle "Set the quantity and price for this quote. Your quote is kept as it is." does not say what "kept as it is" means; **Unit (optional)** is asked for a product that already has a unit; the order is Product, Size, Quantity, Unit, Price, Description, Discount, then Extras, so the thing that matters most for a cake (its size and extras) is above the fold but the total is not.
7. **Terms:** the new-term form says "Optional." twice in the Title field; 18 example chips.

## T7. "Coming soon" cards in the middle of real forms
There are 14 placeholders. Several sit **between real fields**: "Customer agrees to the terms" (between Terms and How to pay on the quote), "Only on certain products" (inside the term form), "Put returned items back in stock" (inside the credit note sheet), "Email it to your customer" (invoice page), "Stock" (end of Details). That is by design (thin slices), but a form that mixes things you can do with things you cannot makes every section feel less certain. **Proposed:** keep them visible, but at the end of the screen, in one quiet "Coming later" group.

## T8. Smaller consistency items
- **Add verbs and articles:** `Add item` / `Add an item`, `Add material` / `Add a material`, `Add customer` / `Add a customer`, `Add new product` / `Add product`, `Add your time` / `Add other cost`. Rule: on a list page header, `Add <noun>`; inside a form, `Add a <noun>`.
- **"(optional)"** is on 28 labels. In a section that is itself optional it adds noise. Keep it only where the form would otherwise look required.
- **Numbered headings** "Material 1", "Your time 1", "Other cost 1", "Size 1 price": numbers carry nothing; name the thing.
- **Dates** show the browser's own format (month first in this test browser); fine for real phones with a South African locale.

## What already works (keep)
The product form's tabs and the pinned bar; the plus-icon add buttons; short empty states with one action; the sheets (title, one-line context, primary then cancel); status chips; the quote preview with the real document; the activity logs; Share/Download on a phone.
