# South Africa (ZA): consumer-law points behind a maker's quote policies

> **Applies to businesses whose country is South Africa (`country_code` = `ZA`) and to no one else.** The example policies a maker can start from (changes after sign-off, cancellation for made-to-order goods and for bookings, handmade variations, allergies and handling, fittings and dates, repairs and aftercare) are South African and live in the South African locale pack (`app/src/lib/locale/za.ts`). Other countries get their own research and their own starters. **This is research to inform wording, not legal advice. A South African legal adviser should check the starters before they are relied on** (same item as POPIA in `docs/security-notes.md`).

Read 2026-10-05 from the **Consumer Protection Act 68 of 2008** (Government Gazette 32186, https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/32186_467.pdf). The text was extracted from the PDF locally and read directly; quotations below are from that text. **Not read:** the Consumer Protection Act Regulations (2011), the National Consumer Commission's explanatory notes (for example on reasonable cancellation charges), and the Minister's thresholds; the points that depend on them are marked.

## What the Act says, and what it means for a policy

1. **Who it protects (section 5).** It applies to every transaction in the Republic with a "consumer", except where the consumer is a juristic person whose asset value or annual turnover "equals or exceeds the threshold value determined by the Minister in terms of section 6" (the threshold figure is not in the Act and was not read). A quote to a small business is therefore treated like one to a person; a big company is outside.
2. **Cancelling an advance order or booking (section 17).**
   - **17(1): "This section does not apply to a franchise agreement, or in respect of any special-order goods."** Section 1 defines special-order goods as "goods that a supplier expressly or implicitly was required or expected to procure, create or alter specifically to satisfy the consumer's requirements". **On my reading of that definition, a made-to-order cake, a bespoke ring or a dress made to measure is special-order goods, so the section 17 cancellation rules do not apply to it** (a legal adviser should confirm, including for goods that are partly standard and partly customised). What a maker may charge on cancellation of made-to-order goods is a matter of the contract and general fairness (points 6 and 7), not of section 17.
   - For **services and bookings** (a workshop, a fitting, an event booking, a cake decorating class), section 17 applies: the consumer may cancel (17(2)); the supplier may require "a reasonable deposit in advance" and "impose a reasonable charge for cancellation" (17(3)); a charge "is unreasonable if it exceeds a fair amount in the circumstances, having regard to (a) the nature of the goods or services that were reserved or booked; (b) the length of notice of cancellation provided by the consumer; (c) the reasonable potential for the service provider, acting diligently, to find an alternative consumer between the time of receiving the cancellation notice and the time of the cancelled reservation; and (d) the general practice of the relevant industry" (17(4)); and **no cancellation fee may be charged if the consumer cannot honour the booking "because of the death or hospitalisation of the person for whom, or for whose benefit the booking, reservation or order was made"** (17(5)).
   - So the cancellation policy needs **two kinds of starter wording**: one for made-to-order goods (charge cost-based: deposit, materials already bought, work already done, with the working shown) and one for services and bookings (reasonable charge by notice given, with the death or hospitalisation exception).
3. **Changing the price or the date (sections 15(4), 19).**
   - 15(4): where an estimate was given "for any service, or goods and services, the supplier may not charge the consumer a price ... that exceeds the estimate, unless after providing the estimate (a) the service provider has informed the consumer of the additional estimated charges; and (b) the consumer has authorised the work to continue." (also in `vat-and-documents.md`). So "any change after sign-off is re-quoted" is the right shape, and the customer must authorise the new price.
   - 19(2): unless the agreement expressly provides otherwise, the supplier must deliver "on the agreed date and at the agreed time, if any, or otherwise within a reasonable time". 19(6): if the supplier tenders delivery "at a location, on a date or at a time other than as agreed", the consumer may accept it, require the agreed date if it has not passed, or "cancel the agreement without penalty". **A policy saying a change "may move the delivery date" must therefore say the new date is agreed with the customer**, not set by the maker alone.
4. **Faulty goods: the implied warranty (sections 55, 56, 51).**
   - 55(2): every consumer has a right to goods that are reasonably suitable for their usual purpose, "of good quality, in good working order and free of any defects", and "useable and durable for a reasonable period of time". 55(6): this does not apply to a transaction if the consumer "has been expressly informed that particular goods were offered in a specific condition" **and** "has expressly agreed to accept the goods in that condition, or knowingly acted in a manner consistent with accepting the goods in that condition". 19(5)(b): for special-order goods the consumer may examine them on delivery to check they "reasonably conform to the material specifications of the special order".
   - 56(2): "Within six months after the delivery of any goods to a consumer, the consumer may return the goods to the supplier, without penalty and at the supplier's risk and expense, if the goods fail to satisfy" section 55, and the supplier must, at the consumer's direction, "repair or replace" or "refund". 56(3): if a repair does not hold within three months, the supplier must replace or refund. 56(4): these rights are "in addition to" any other warranty, including an express one the maker offers.
   - **51(1)(b): a term is prohibited if it "directly or indirectly purports to (i) waive or deprive a consumer of a right in terms of this Act; (ii) avoid a supplier's obligation or duty".** So an aftercare or warranty policy can add to the six-month right (free resizing, cleaning, repairs) but must not take it away, and should say so ("this does not affect your legal rights").
   - **Expected variations** (handmade, natural stones, dye lots, fresh flowers): the way to rely on 55(6) is to say clearly, before the customer agrees, that the item will vary in these ways, and to have the customer agree (accepting the quote after reading it).
5. **Repairs and maintenance of the customer's own property (section 15(1)-(3), `vat-and-documents.md`).** Not built, by decision (founder, 2026-10-02). Noted only because "resizing, repairs, cleaning" in an aftercare policy is the maker repairing their own work, which section 57 covers: "a service provider warrants every new or reconditioned part installed during any repair or maintenance work, and the labour required to install it, for a period of three months after the date of installation or such longer period as the supplier may specify in writing", not applying to misuse, abuse or ordinary wear and tear.
6. **Limiting liability or shifting risk (sections 49, 51, 61, 48, 22).**
   - **49(1): a notice or term that "limit[s] in any way the risk or liability of the supplier", "constitute[s] an assumption of risk or liability by the consumer", "impose[s] an obligation on the consumer to indemnify the supplier", or "be[s] an acknowledgement of any fact by the consumer" must be drawn to the consumer's attention in a way that meets 49(3) to (5):** in plain language (section 22: understandable "without undue effort" to a consumer "with average literacy skills", helped by "illustrations, examples, headings or other aids to reading"), "in a conspicuous manner and form that is likely to attract the attention of an ordinarily alert consumer", and "before the earlier of the time at which the consumer enters into the transaction or agreement ... or is required or expected to offer consideration", with "an adequate opportunity ... to receive and comprehend" it. **This is why policies should be headed and readable on the quote, not hidden in small print, and why the quote (which comes before the customer agrees or pays a deposit) is the right place for them.** For a risk that is unusual, not obvious, or could cause serious injury or death (49(2), 58) the customer must also assent by signing or initialling or acting consistently with acknowledgement.
   - 48(2)(b): a transaction is unfair if subject to such a term or notice and either the term is "unfair, unreasonable, unjust or unconscionable" or its "fact, nature and effect ... was not drawn to the attention of the consumer" as section 49 requires.
   - 51(1)(c)(i): a term is prohibited if it purports to "limit or exempt a supplier ... from liability for any loss directly or indirectly attributable to the gross negligence of the supplier". **A liability cap can never cover gross negligence.** 51(1)(h)(ii): a term is prohibited if it "requires the consumer to forfeit any money to the supplier ... to which the supplier is not entitled in terms of this Act or any other law" (relevant to deposit-forfeiture wording).
   - 61(1): the producer, importer, distributor or retailer is liable for harm caused by "supplying any unsafe goods", "a product failure, defect or hazard", or "inadequate instructions or warnings provided to the consumer pertaining to any hazard arising from or associated with the use of any goods", "irrespective of whether the harm resulted from any negligence". **Giving real handling, storage and allergen information is part of what protects the maker**, which is why "client responsibilities" should be filled in with real instructions, not left generic. (Food-labelling and allergen rules under other law were not researched.)

## What this means for the build (see `docs/plans/quote-policies.md`)
- Policies are shown as **titled sections** on the quote, in plain language, before the customer agrees (decision, founder 2026-10-05).
- **Cancellation** has two examples (made-to-order goods; services and bookings).
- The **changes** example says the new price and date are agreed with the customer.
- The **repairs and aftercare** example adds to, and never removes, the customer's legal rights, and offers no liability cap.
- Every example marks what to fill in with [square brackets] and is labelled as a prompt, not legal advice.
- Not built or not verified: the thresholds and regulations listed at the top, the National Consumer Commission's notes on reasonable charges, food and allergen labelling law, and signing or initialling for unusual risks (a customer acceptance step arrives with online quotes).

## Examples added for business types (2026-10-06): what is and is not backed by the research above
These six examples come with the "What do you make?" step (`docs/plans/business-type.md`). A South African legal adviser must check them with the rest.
- **Storage and serving (food), Safe use and sensitive skin (candles, soap, beauty):** rest on the points above about instructions and warnings (a maker can be responsible when harm comes from faulty goods or missing instructions or warnings; section 61 as noted under the allergies example). They add instructions, they do not limit liability.
- **Engraving and personalising, Measurements and alterations:** rest on the special-order-goods reading (point 2) and the six-month faulty-goods right (point 4). Engraving says "if we make a mistake, we will put it right" and "this does not affect your legal rights"; measurements offers a free alteration "on top of your legal rights" with no time limit. They add to the customer's rights and never remove them. A proof-approval line reads a little like the customer acknowledging a fact (section 49 asks that such terms be conspicuous and in plain language), so the adviser should look at it. **The special-order reading is still unconfirmed.**
- **Seasonal substitutions (flowers):** the "say it before the customer agrees, and be specific" point (variations, point 4), plus the point that a change is agreed with the customer (point 3): the text offers to agree a change on request. It does not excuse flowers in poor condition.
- **Collection and delivery (food):** the agreed date and time point (points 3 and 19(2)). **Not researched:** who carries the risk once a courier has the order, and food transport law. It is on the adviser checklist.
